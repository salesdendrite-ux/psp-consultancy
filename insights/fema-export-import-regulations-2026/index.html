FEMA Export-Import Regulations 2026: What Changed

By PSP ConsultancyPublished 2026-10-06Updated 2026-10-067 min read
In shortThe Foreign Exchange Management (Export and Import of Goods and Services) Regulations, 2026 took effect on 1 October 2026, replacing the 2015 export regulations. After RBI's amendment of 22 September 2026, exporters generally have nine months to realise export proceeds, or twelve months where exports are invoiced or settled in rupees. Service exporters file the Export Declaration Form monthly.

How we got here

DateDevelopment
13 January 2026RBI notifies the unified Export and Import of Goods and Services Regulations, 2026 (FEMA 23(R)/2026-RB), published in the Gazette on 15 January 2026, to take effect from 1 October 2026
5 June 2026An amendment to the 2015 export regulations brings the realisation period back from 15 months to 9 months
22 September 2026RBI amends the 2026 regulations (FEMA 23(R)/(1)/2026-RB), cutting the realisation periods before they take effect; published in the Gazette on 24 September 2026
1 October 2026The 2026 regulations take effect and supersede the 2015 regulations, subject to savings

What changed

The 2026 regulations consolidate the rules on exports and imports of goods, services and software into a single instrument. They replace the Foreign Exchange Management (Export of Goods & Services) Regulations, 2015 and are supported by RBI's directions to authorised dealer (AD) banks. The most visible changes for exporters are the realisation period, a leaner reporting process for service exports and simpler handling of small transactions.

Realisation periods

ExportPeriod to realise proceedsCounted from
Goods and software, in foreign currency9 monthsDate of shipment
Services, in foreign currency9 monthsInvoice date
Exports invoiced or settled in rupees12 monthsAs above

The 22 September amendment cut the periods from 15 to 9 months, and from 18 to 12 months for rupee exports. Banks can extend the period on request, with reasons. Work out the date for a specific shipment or invoice with our export realisation date calculator.

EDF for service exports

Service exporters file the Export Declaration Form (EDF) within 30 days of the end of the month in which the invoice was raised. One form can cover all of that month's service exports, which removes the need for a form per invoice.

Simpler rules for small transactions

For shipping bills or invoices up to ₹10 lakh, banks can close monitoring-system entries and allow reduced export value on the exporter's declaration, which reduces follow-up on small shortfalls.

Transition from the 2015 regulations

The September amendment also kept Caution List exporters under the earlier orders that apply to them, and let AD banks handle transactions undertaken before 1 October 2026 that previously needed RBI approval. If you have old, unrealised shipments or invoices, ask your bank which regime applies to each.

Exporter checklist

  1. Re-date your receivables. Build a list of every open shipment or invoice with its realisation deadline under the 9 or 12 month rule.
  2. Flag anything before 5 June 2026. An earlier, longer window may have applied. Confirm with your bank.
  3. Set up the EDF calendar for service exports: one filing per month, within 30 days of month end.
  4. Check your contracts. Payment terms longer than nine months now need a bank extension request, with reasons, before the date passes.
  5. Talk to your AD bank early. Ask how it will treat pre-October cases and what documents it wants for small-value closures.
  6. Keep evidence ready. Shipping bills, invoices, FIRCs or bank advices and correspondence on shortfalls.

Frequently asked questions

What are the FEMA export-import regulations, 2026?

They are the RBI's unified regulations (Notification FEMA 23(R)/2026-RB) covering exports and imports of goods, services and software. They replace the 2015 export regulations and took effect on 1 October 2026.

How long do exporters have to realise export proceeds?

Nine months from the date of shipment for goods or the invoice date for services, and twelve months where exports are invoiced or settled in rupees. Banks can extend the period on request, with reasons.

What did the 22 September 2026 amendment change?

It cut the realisation periods from 15 to 9 months, and from 18 to 12 months for rupee exports. It also kept Caution List exporters under earlier orders and let banks handle pre-October cases that needed RBI approval.

Are there simpler rules for small transactions?

Yes. For shipping bills or invoices up to ₹10 lakh, banks can close monitoring-system entries and allow reduced export value on the exporter's declaration.

When must service exporters file the Export Declaration Form?

Within 30 days of the end of the month in which the invoice was raised. One form can cover all of that month's service exports.
